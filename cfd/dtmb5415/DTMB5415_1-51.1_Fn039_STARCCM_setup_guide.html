DTMB 5415 calm-water resistance in STAR-CCM+

Model scale 1:51.1 · Fn = 0.39 · overset mesh · DFBI free sinkage & trim · VOF · prepared 26 Sep 2026

Read first. The published DTMB 5415 models are INSEAN 2340 (1:24.824, LPP 5.720 m), IIHR/DTMB 5512 (1:46.588, 3.048 m, used at the Tokyo 2015 and Gothenburg 2010 workshops) and FORCE (1:35.48, 4.002 m). I don't know of a 1:51.1 model, so every number here is scaled from the full-scale hull (LPP = 142.0 m) to your scale. If your IGES file was already scaled to another model, check LPP in the geometry and recompute (all mesh sizes below scale linearly with L, and y+ heights scale roughly as L0.1/√L at fixed Fn). Hull particulars marked ⚑ are from memory of the workshop documentation. Check them against your geometry's hydrostatics before running.

2. Domain and boundary conditions

Half domain with a symmetry plane at y = 0 (valid because only heave and pitch are free). Pick any axis orientation; only the extents matter. ITTC 7.5-03-02-03 recommends inlet 1–2 L, outlet 3–5 L, side 1.5–2 L, bottom 1–2 L.

Wave damping: VOF wave damping on outlet and side boundaries, damping length 3.0 m (≈1.1 λ), vertical damping on, default coefficients (f1 = 10, f2 = 10, n = 2). Alternative: VOF wave forcing to the flat wave over the same length, which also handles the inlet.

Matching rules:

  • Cells on both sides of the overlap must be the same size (ratio ≤ 1.2) in every direction, including the free-surface Δz. This is why the background near-hull box and free-surface band use exactly the overset sizes.
  • At least 4–5 cells between the hull (prism layer top) and the overset boundary, and at least 4–5 overlapping background cells across the boundary. Nothing inside a gap narrower than that (watch the gap under the keel).
  • The background refinement must cover the overset box at every position it can reach. Allow for sinkage and trim: at the ends a trim of 0.5° moves the box ±12 mm vertically.
  • Overset interface: background ↔ overset region, interpolation "Linear" (distance-weighted is fine for coarse), Hole cutting default. Check the "Overset cell status" scalar: acceptor cells must form a continuous layer, and no "inactive" cells can touch the hull.

  • Check the residual plot after about 2 L/U. If continuity or the volume fraction stalls around 1e-3 on most steps and the cap of 20 is hit every time, the time step is too large for this grid. Reduce Δt, for example from 0.005 to 0.0035 L/U, rather than raising the cap.
  • In unsteady VOF runs with DFBI, continuity is usually the last residual to reach 1e-4, especially just after the body release and during the ramp. Some steps hitting the cap during 1–4 s is normal.
  • The residual target is not the resistance convergence test. Also require RT, sinkage and trim to change by less than 0.5–1% over the last 5–10 L/U.

8. DFBI setup and ramp

  • 6-DOF body on the hull boundaries. Because of the symmetry plane enter half values: mass 30.77 kg, Iyy 14.85 kg·m² (Ixx, Izz any positive value since those DOFs are fixed). CG at LCG ≈ 1.370 m forward of AP ⚑, KG = 0.148 m ⚑ above keel, y = 0.
  • Degrees of freedom: translation in z + rotation about y only (use a 1-DOF translation + 1-DOF rotation constraint, or the "Free motion" options in newer versions).
  • Start position: even keel at T = 0.1204 m. Check that hydrostatic heave force balances the weight in the first steps (a mismatch means mass, draft or water density is off).
  • Release time 1.0 s (≈0.75 L/U) so the start-up transient in the flow is not passed to the body.
  • Ramp time 3.0 s (≈2.2 L/U): the DFBI forces are blended in linearly, which avoids a large heave overshoot.
  • 6-DOF solver: update the body every inner iteration; 6-DOF under-relaxation ~0.5 if the body motion oscillates between iterations; turn on "Enable 6-DOF solver" only after the release time (the release time does this).
  • If you ramp the inflow instead (velocity field function from 0 to V over 2 L/U), keep the release time after the ramp finishes.
  • Towing force: fixed surge means the drag is taken at the CG. A towing tank tows at the tow point (usually around the CG height or on the shaft line). The difference in pitch moment is small; for a closer match add an external force equal to RT at the tow-point height.

Monitors: RT (total, and pressure/shear split) on the hull, sinkage of the CG (translation z), trim angle, and the overset cell status.

9. Resistance reduction per ITTC

ITTC 7.5-02-02-01 (resistance test) and 7.5-02-03-01.4 (1978 performance prediction):

  • CT = RT / (½ρV²S) with S = static wetted surface at T (1.1296 m² for the full hull, i.e. 2 × RT,half). State whether S is static or dynamic.
  • CF = 0.075/(log Re − 2)² = 3.400e-3 at model scale.
  • Form factor (1+k): run a double-body (no free surface) RANS at the same Re, or use a Prohaska plot. For a transom-stern hull like 5415 the ITTC 2017 recommendation is to get k from low-speed or double-body runs.
  • CW = CT − (1+k)CF.
  • Full scale: CTS = (1+k)CFS + ΔCF + CA + CW + CAAS, with ReS = 1.74e9, CFS = 1.431e-3, ΔCF = 0.044[(kS/L)1/3 − 10 Re−1/3] + 0.000125 = 2.07e-4 for kS = 150 µm.
Comparing with published data: INSEAN 2340 and IIHR 5512 were tested at larger scales, so their CT is at a different Re. Compare CW (or CR) and sinkage/trim non-dimensionalised by L, not CT directly. Fn = 0.39 is not a workshop condition (workshops used 0.28 and 0.41), so interpolate from the published CT–Fn curves.

10. Grid and time-step convergence plan (ITTC 7.5-03-01-01)

Three systematically refined grids with ratio r = √2 applied to every size control (base size, all volumetric controls, surface sizes). Refine the time step with the same ratio so the Courant number stays constant. With wall functions keep the first-cell thickness fixed (y+ stays in the log layer) and refine tangentially; state this in the report because it is not strictly systematic.

GridBaseKelvin wedge Δx/ΔzHull surfaceΔtRough cell count (half domain) ⚑
G3 coarse113 mm28.3 / 3.54 mm14.1 mm9.65 ms≈1.2 M
G2 medium80 mm20 / 2.5 mm10 mm6.82 ms≈3.5 M
G1 fine56.6 mm14.1 / 1.77 mm7.1 mm4.83 ms≈10 M

⚑ cell counts are estimates (each √2 step multiplies the count by ~2.8). Your actual count depends on how large the Kelvin wedge is.

Procedure for each quantity S (CT, sinkage, trim):

  1. ε21 = S2 − S1, ε32 = S3 − S2; convergence ratio RG = ε21/ε32. 0 < RG < 1 monotonic; RG < 0 oscillatory; RG > 1 divergent.
  2. Monotonic: pG = ln(ε32/ε21)/ln r; δ*RE = ε21/(rpG − 1); CG = (rpG − 1)/(rpest − 1) with pest = 2.
  3. UG = [9.6(1 − CG)² + 1.1]|δ*RE| if |1 − CG| < 0.125, else [2|1 − CG| + 1]|δ*RE|. Oscillatory: UG = ½(SU − SL).
  4. Repeat with Δt ratio √2 on the medium grid for UT. USN = √(UI² + UG² + UT²).
  5. Validation: E = D − S; validated at the UV level if |E| < UV = √(UD² + USN²). UD from your tank data or the published EFD uncertainty (≈1% of CT for the 5415 data sets).

The ITTC 2017 update and Eça & Hoekstra (2014) recommend least-squares fits over 4+ grids when three grids scatter. Add a G0 (r = 2 coarser than G1) if RG does not come out monotonic.

  • Lateral: your 30 mm already meets the ≥ 80 cells per wavelength rule, and STAR G3 (28.3 mm) is essentially the same lateral resolution. So the coarse STAR grid is the like-for-like comparison with your RANS3D result in x and y.
  • Vertical: 10 mm is 3–4× coarser than the STAR bands. I recommend keeping the finer Δz in STAR because HRIC VOF smears the interface over about 2–3 cells. With 10 mm cells that is a 20–30 mm interface, which is comparable to the wave amplitudes near the hull at this model size. How sharp the interface stays in RANS3D depends on its interface method (level set or VOF), which I don't know. So the 10 mm may have been adequate there without carrying over to STAR VOF.
  • Optional solver-matched run: to separate solver differences from grid differences, run one extra STAR case with the RANS3D sizes (30 mm lateral, 10 mm vertical in the same refined-region extents) and the same time step. Compare CT, sinkage, trim and wave cuts from STAR-matched, STAR G3–G1 and RANS3D.
  • If your RANS3D refined region's extents (length ahead/behind the hull, width, height band) differ from the Kelvin wedge and free-surface band in Section 3, use the larger of the two so both solvers resolve the same part of the wave field.

11. Pre-run checklist

  • Geometry LPP = 2.779 m, closed and watertight; draft line at z = 0 after placing the hull at T.
  • Hydrostatics: buoyancy of the half hull = 30.77 × 9.81 = 301.9 N at T.
  • Overset: no orphan cells, ≥ 4 cells overlap, equal cell sizes in the overlap (check with a section through the free surface).
  • Wall y+ within the target band after 5 L/U.
  • Free-surface isosurface stays inside the 2.5 mm band; no reflections from the side/outlet (look at the wave pattern at 20 L/U).
  • Residuals drop ≥ 2 orders per time step; RT, sinkage and trim flat over the averaging window.
